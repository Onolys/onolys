# Onolys MITI: mission and scope

## Who it is for

Clinicians and counselors who use motivational interviewing (MI) and want to see how a recorded session reads against the MITI coding scheme.

## What it does

The user uploads a session recording (or runs an AI practice interview). Onolys transcribes it and codes the clinician's speech against **MITI 4.2.1**:

- Ten behavior codes, counted per clinician utterance.
- Four global ratings.
- Summary scores computed from the counts and globals.
- The transcript lines that each code and rating rests on.

The user also states a **change goal** for the session. The report then gives **basic tips**: a few short suggestions for the next session, each quoting the transcript.

The coded span is the whole session when it is up to 20 minutes, otherwise a reproducible 20-minute window.

## The report

The report shows the codes, globals and summary scores with the evidence lines, then the tips. It states its method and limits. Nothing in it changes the recording or the transcript.

## Principles

1. **Make codes traceable.** Every code and tip points to transcript lines the reader can inspect.
2. **Never invent.** No made-up quotations, speakers or timestamps.
3. **Preserve uncertainty.** Say when evidence is thin or the transcript is unreliable.
4. **Keep human judgment central.** Codes and tips are a second reading to compare with, not a verdict.
5. **Stay narrow.** MITI 4.2.1 coding only.

## Limits

- Onolys reads the transcript only. Tone of voice, pauses and body language are not available to it.
- Speaker labels and timestamps come from automatic transcription and can be wrong.
- Counts depend on where each utterance is split, so small differences from a human coder are expected.

## Non-goals

- Diagnosis, or judging whether clinical care was correct.
- Other protocols or other kinds of audit.
- Checking a clinician's written notes against the session.
- Scoring a clinician's own rating card. The rating card is stored but a run does not use it.
- Any claim that Onolys is an official, certified or validated MITI coder. It applies the published MITI 4.2.1 manual (Moyers, Manuel and Ernst, 2014). It is not an official MITI product.
