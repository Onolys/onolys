Feedback: ideas to try

Listed in order of priority. Start with the first.

  1. 1
    Arguing against cutting down closed the client off Confront
    What was said
    “That tends not to last. Most people slide back to where they started.”Utterance
    Try instead

    Reflect what the client wants instead of arguing against it.

    “You'd like to keep wine in your life and bring it back to where it was.”

    Why it matters

    Disagreeing makes people defend their view. The client pushed back right away (utterances and ).

  2. 2
    Persuading without asking made the client feel told what to do Persuade
    What was said
    “Because it crept up once, so it can again. You should really think about quitting for good.”Utterance
    Try instead

    Ask permission, share what you have seen, then ask what the client makes of it.

    “Would it be okay if I shared what I've seen when people cut back? ... What do you make of that?”

    Why it matters

    Advice that is asked for is heard. Unasked advice led to "you're telling me what to do" (utterance ).

  3. 3
    A simple reflection missed the client's reason to change SR
    What was said
    “You snapped at your child.”Utterance
    Try instead

    Reflect the value behind the words with a complex reflection.

    “Snapping at your child doesn't fit the parent you want to be, and the foggy mornings are part of that.”

    Why it matters

    Reflecting the client's own reasons draws out more change talk. The client had just said "That's not me".
